# Ads Bot: guarantee and lock-in

## 1. Guarantee: yes, but a simple one

Offer a first-month satisfaction refund, not a results guarantee.

- **What is guaranteed:** "Try it for 30 days of live ads. If you're not happy, for any reason, we refund your ₪249." Do not promise lead counts, cost per lead or positions. Those depend on his market and on whether he answers the phone, and a numeric promise invites disputes.
- **Window:** 30 days from the first ad served, not from payment. Otherwise setup delays eat the trial.
- **Refund:** WAO's ₪249 only, stated in the same sentence: "Ad spend goes to Google, not to us, so we can't refund it."
- **Conditions (keep to three):**
  - Ads actually ran in the window at the ₪1,500 pace.
  - One refund per business.
  - He asks within 7 days of the window ending.
- **Conditions to drop:** "tracking installed" and "ads approved" are WAO's job. If WAO fails to get ads live, refund automatically.

**Why this shape:** this buyer has been burned by agencies, and a guarantee with five conditions looks like the fine print that burned him. The exposure is small: ₪249 per refund plus setup effort. The setup-and-run abuse is handled by the page policy below, not by more conditions.

**Legal points for the lawyer to confirm** (I am not citing statutes):
- The guarantee must be worded as on top of the statutory 14-day cancellation right (and the 4-month extension), never instead of it.
- "Iron-clad" or "unconditional" wording next to any conditions may count as misleading. I would use "money-back commitment, first month" and list the three conditions beside it.
- Whether business customers count as "consumers" at all for the cancellation right. WAO's terms grant it anyway, so keep it uniform.
- Refund mechanics through Takbull: credit invoice, VAT and timing.
- Dror should check Google's third-party partner rules on guarantee claims before the copy ships.

## 2. Lock-in: tie the pages to the subscription, openly, with a buyout

**His, always:** the Google Ads account, campaigns, ads, keywords, conversion history and data. This is already promised, and it is unenforceable otherwise because he owns the account.

**WAO's, included while subscribed:**
- Landing pages on `{slug}.wao.co.il`
- Templates
- Fraud Blocker
- Weekly digest
- Ongoing optimisation

**On cancel**, mirror Site-Bot (terms §8) so there is one rule across products:
- Pages stay live for 30 days, then go offline.
- Warn him clearly that ads pointing at offline pages will stop working, so he needs to repoint or pause them.
- Offer a one-time buyout to export the pages or move them to his own domain. I suggest about ₪750–1,000, free after 12 paid months; your memory notes show Site-Bot already settled on buyout/free-after-12-months.

**Is it ethical?** Yes, if he reads it before paying. It is like a rented tool that goes back when the rental ends. It becomes hostage-taking only if he discovers it at cancellation, so put it in the FAQ and the terms.

**How much it protects you:** not much. A determined DIYer can rebuild a page in an afternoon. This persona has near-zero time and rarely goes DIY. The real retention is what stops when he leaves: optimisation, Fraud Blocker and the digest. Sell those, not the lock.

**Conflict with "the account stays yours":** none, if the copy is precise. Suggested message for Tamar to write: "The account, campaigns and data are yours, and leaving takes one click. The landing pages run on our system and are part of the subscription; if you leave you can buy them out." I did not re-read the live /google-ads copy. If any line implies "everything we build is yours," it must be fixed before launch.

**Do the two policies reinforce each other?** Yes. The guarantee makes it safe to start, and the page policy makes refund-and-run unprofitable without adding guarantee conditions.

## Recommendation

1. Launch with the 30-day satisfaction refund: ₪249 only, three conditions, clock starts at the first live ad.
2. Keep landing pages as a subscription asset: 30 days after cancel, then offline, with a paid buyout that becomes free after 12 months.
3. Disclose both on the sales page and in the terms, and have the lawyer review them in the same pass as the Site-Bot terms.

## What would change my mind

- **Refund rate above about 15–20% in the first 20–30 clients:** tighten the guarantee or fix onboarding.
- **Lawyer says the guarantee wording creates liability beyond the ₪249:** fall back to promoting only the statutory right plus "cancel any time."
- **Prospects on the free call object to the pages policy:** move to "pages are yours after 3 paid months."
- **Evidence that clients do clone and leave:** raise the buyout price rather than hide the terms.

## Dror: Google policy check (web-verified 2026-11-11): OK with CHANGES
1. **Refund:** a refund of WAO's ₪249 fee is fine. Describe it as a refund of the service fee, never as a guarantee of leads, position or results. Name the 3 conditions next to the offer, not only in the terms.
2. **Partner badge:** only on WAO's own site, linked to WAO's public Partner profile, with no implied Google endorsement. Keep it visually apart from the refund block. Never on client landing pages ({slug}.wao.co.il) or invoices.
3. **Disclosures the page must state:**
   - the ₪249 fee is separate from the ad spend, which goes directly to Google;
   - each client gets his own Google Ads account, owned by him and linked to WAO's MCC;
   - he can see clicks, impressions and total cost at any time;
   - there is a written agreement.
4. **₪1,500 floor:** OK to state up front. Say it goes to Google, not to WAO, and call it a minimum, not a recommendation. That's about ₪50/day; it's thin for expensive niches.
5. **Avoid:** "guaranteed results", "first place"/"top of Google", "always visible", "unlimited clicks", "official Google partner", "approved, recommended or certified by Google", "in partnership with Google", "iron-clad", "unconditional". A plain "Google Partner" is fine.
