# Video scripts — Dash animated YouTube ads (round 1, 2026-11-12)

Six short animated ads for WAO's two bots, to be voiced now with ElevenLabs and kept as options for YouTube remarketing later. You write ALL the Hebrew: Dash's lines, the on-screen cartoon banners, captions, and Eitan's closing line. Comedy in native spoken Israeli Hebrew. A translated joke is a dead joke. The concept below is in English as information only; take the idea, not its wording.

## Products (facts you may state)
- **Ads Bot:** Google ads for a tradesman, run by a bot, ₪249 + VAT a month. It opens a new Google Ads account in his name. It learns from the enquiries he marks as real and closed, not from clicks. Every enquiry and what it cost, in front of him each week. Agency management typically starts at about ₪1,500 a month.
- **Site Bot:** the complete organic Google system for a one-man trade business, ₪199 + VAT a month: a site laid out as his tree of services, his Google profile kept matching it, and help getting his customers to leave reviews. Free check: he types his business name and sees what customers see. Never call it a website builder; never shrink the site.
- **Eitan:** WAO's founder, in digital marketing since 2006. He voices the closing line himself.

## Concept (from the strategist)
## 3. Our own concept: "The phone that doesn't ring"

Names are English placeholders. The Hebrew name, every joke and every spoken line are Tamar's (via waocopy), then Noa, then your spot-check.

### The character
- **Who:** the tradesman's own work phone. Rugged, scuffed, in a thick orange rubber case with plaster dust in the corners and a hairline crack across one corner. Working name: **Dash**, because it lives on a magnet mount on the van's dashboard.
- **Face:** two eyes and a mouth drawn on its screen, like a simple display animation.
- **Personality:** deadpan, loyal, slightly tired. It sees every click, every search and every missed customer. It never mocks its owner; it mocks what is being done to him.
- **Signature device:** its jokes arrive as cartoon notification banners on its own screen, so every joke also reads on mute.

### The world
- **Place:** the dashboard of a work van, parked between jobs. Coffee cup, receipts, a spirit level, a jar of coins.
- **The owner:** never fully seen. Hands, forearms, a tool belt, a voice from off-screen. Any plumber, electrician or AC technician can see himself. Singular male address.
- **The enemies, made visible:**
  - Ads Bot: **click-flies**, small cursor-shaped insects that each eat a coin and leave without a sound.
  - Site Bot: **the ghost van**. The van and its owner are see-through to everyone searching on the pavement.

### Why this design
- **The phone is the scoreboard.** Both bots are judged by whether the phone rings.
- **Real product screens appear naturally** on a phone, so the trust element is built into the character.
- **It is cheap and consistent to animate.** A rigid object with a screen-face has no body to drift between episodes. Its mouth can be driven directly by the audio track, which removes the hardest technical risk (Hebrew lip-sync).
- **It looks nothing like Contabo's mascot** and borrows from no film or game.

### Episode format
- **Hook (0–3s):** one impossible image plus one notification. Brand mark in the corner from the first frame.
- **Escalation:** the same gag, twice, bigger.
- **Turn:** Dash says the true thing plainly.
- **Product entry:** Dash's screen switches from cartoon face to the real product screen. Your real voice delivers one line. End card.
- **Never:** the phone ringing off the hook at the end, a counter of leads, or a "before and after". Those imply a result.


## The six episodes (strategist beat sheets)
### Ads Bot episodes (enemy: paying for clicks that never ring)

**A1. "The meter"**
- **Hook (0–3s):** Dash's screen is a taxi meter. Each click sound adds shekels. Dash stares at the camera, silent. Notification: another click, no call.
- **15s beats:**
  - 0–3: hook.
  - 3–7: the meter speeds up; coins leave the jar on their own.
  - 7–10: the owner's hand picks Dash up, checks for missed calls, finds none. Dash delivers the turn (intent: "they all clicked, nobody rang").
  - 10–15: product entry and end card.
- **30s beats:**
  - 0–3: hook.
  - 3–10: three quick clicks, each shown as a cursor that looks around and leaves.
  - 10–16: the owner asks who these people were; Dash shrugs.
  - 16–20: the turn.
  - 20–27: real product screen, a lead being marked as real by the owner's thumb.
  - 27–30: end card with price.
- **Product enters on:** intent "Ads Bot learns from the leads you mark as real, not from clicks."

**A2. "Click-flies"**
- **Hook (0–3s):** a cloud of cursor-shaped flies circles the coin jar. Dash swats one with its own charging cable.
- **15s beats:**
  - 0–3: hook.
  - 3–8: every fly takes a coin and leaves; Dash counts them out loud, bored.
  - 8–10: the turn (intent: "lots of visitors, zero customers").
  - 10–15: product entry.
- **30s beats:**
  - 0–3: hook.
  - 3–9: the swarm grows; the owner sprays, they come back.
  - 9–15: one fly stops, looks like it might call, then leaves.
  - 15–20: the turn.
  - 20–27: real screen, the owner marks which enquiries were real jobs.
  - 27–30: end card.
- **Product enters on:** intent "tell it which calls were real work, and it steers the budget toward more like those."

**A3. "The report"**
- **Hook (0–3s):** a glossy report slaps onto the windscreen from outside, full of big green arrows. Dash reads it upside down.
- **15s beats:**
  - 0–3: hook.
  - 3–8: confetti for "clicks" and "impressions"; Dash asks which line is a customer; the confetti stops in mid-air.
  - 8–10: the turn.
  - 10–15: product entry with price.
- **30s beats:**
  - 0–3: hook.
  - 3–10: three reports pile up, each shinier.
  - 10–16: the owner asks how many called; silence, one cricket.
  - 16–20: the turn.
  - 20–27: real screen plus the price line.
  - 27–30: end card.
- **Product enters on:** intent "one number that matters: leads you confirmed. ₪249 + VAT a month."
- **Check before use:** any comparison to agency prices ("typically from about ₪1,500") must be worded as typical, be supportable with evidence, and get Dror's review. Put it on screen as text, not in the joke.

### Site Bot episodes (enemy: invisible on Google despite great work and reviews)

**S1. "Ghost van"**
- **Hook (0–3s):** a woman on the pavement searches on her phone and walks straight through the see-through van. Dash watches her pass.
- **15s beats:**
  - 0–3: hook.
  - 3–8: two more people walk through; one puts a coffee down "on" the bonnet and it falls.
  - 8–10: the turn (intent: "years of good work, and they cannot see us").
  - 10–15: product entry.
- **30s beats:**
  - 0–3: hook.
  - 3–9: the walk-throughs.
  - 9–15: the owner waves; nobody reacts; Dash notes that someone two streets away got the job.
  - 15–20: the turn.
  - 20–27: real screens, the service-tree site next to the business profile, saying the same thing.
  - 27–30: end card.
- **Product enters on:** intent "Site Bot builds your full presence on Google: a site that mirrors every service you do, in step with your business profile."

**S2. "The jar of stars"**
- **Hook (0–3s):** the glovebox opens and a jar of glowing stars rolls out. Notification: compliments received in person, none written anywhere.
- **15s beats:**
  - 0–3: hook.
  - 3–8: each star replays a customer's thank-you at the door as a speech bubble with no sound.
  - 8–10: the turn (intent: "Google has no ears").
  - 10–15: product entry.
- **30s beats:**
  - 0–3: hook.
  - 3–10: the owner adds another star after a job.
  - 10–16: Dash holds the jar up to the windscreen; passers-by cannot see it.
  - 16–20: the turn.
  - 20–27: real screen, the flow that helps a customer write a review.
  - 27–30: end card.
- **Product enters on:** intent "we help your customers put it in writing, where the next customer looks."
- **Check before use:** the customers and their words are generic and fictional, and must never look like quoted testimonials. Yonatan confirms the wording stays inside Google's review rules.

**S3. "The level"**
- **Hook (0–3s):** a spirit level on the dashboard, bubble jammed to one side. Dash shows three labels for the same business, each different.
- **15s beats:**
  - 0–3: hook.
  - 3–8: the site says one trade, the profile another, a customer describes a third; the bubble slides further each time.
  - 8–10: the turn (intent: "three stories, so Google trusts none").
  - 10–15: product entry as the bubble centres.
- **30s beats:**
  - 0–3: hook.
  - 3–10: the three mismatches, one by one.
  - 10–16: the owner straightens a shelf by instinct but cannot straighten this.
  - 16–20: the turn.
  - 20–27: real screens, one service tree with site and profile aligned; the bubble centres.
  - 27–30: end card.
- **Product enters on:** intent "one tree of services, the same story on your site and your profile."

**Positioning guard for all three:** Site Bot is the complete organic Google exposure system for a one-man business. Never "a small website", never a page count, never a ranking promise.

## Rules
- **The joke is on the clicks, the reports and the invisibility, never on the tradesman.** He is a good professional being failed by the system.
- **The cartoon shows only the problem.** The solution line arrives as Dash's screen switches to the real product, then Eitan's closing line. The product line states the mechanism, never a result.
- **Never:**
  - a phone ringing off the hook;
  - a lead counter, a before/after, or a number of calls;
  - first place on Google;
  - a credit amount;
  - real clients;
  - Google logos;
  - banners that look like a real phone notification.
- **Voice:**
  - Dash's spoken lines are short: about 8 words in the hook, 12 at most anywhere;
  - singular male address to the viewer;
  - deadpan, loyal, slightly tired.
- **Notation:** add nikud ONLY on words that could be read two ways when voiced. Give each line twice: `voice` (for TTS, with that nikud) and `display` (clean, for captions).

## Deliver (one JSON object, no markdown; per episode)
For each of A1, A2, A3, S1, S2, S3:
- **hooks:** 3 alternative 0–3s hooks (spoken line + banner text).
- **script_30:** the timed beats.
  - Each beat has: time, what's on screen (English), speaker (Dash / Eitan / none), `voice`, `display`, and banner (if any).
- **script_15:** the 15s cut, in the same shape.
- **eitan_close:** Eitan's closing line, about 6–10 words, plus the end-card text with the price and the CTA.
- **Text assets:** 3 headlines (≤40 chars), 3 long headlines (≤90), 3 descriptions (≤90).

Also give Dash a Hebrew name (3 options, with your pick) and a one-line voice description in English for casting (age, pace, texture).
