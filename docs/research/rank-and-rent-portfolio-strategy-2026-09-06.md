## gadgates.co.il due-diligence gates

The candidate stays HOLD if any evidence is incomplete, contradictory, or not independently reproducible.

1. Current DNS and HTTP: query A/AAAA/CNAME/TXT/MX and resolve from more than one vantage point; record timestamps, status, redirects, TLS, hosting clues, and whether the result is NXDOMAIN, timeout, parked, inactive, or active. Absence is only an unregistered signal.
2. Registrar and ownership: use lawful RDAP/registry signals and a permitted registrar availability check; do not infer ownership from privacy records. Confirm the seller has authority and obtain written transfer permission before any transaction.
3. Wayback continuity: inspect captures across the domain's life, not one snapshot. Record gaps, title/topic continuity, language, ownership clues, and whether the gate topic was genuine.
4. Redirect history: identify historical target domains, chains, expired redirects, doorway patterns, and topic changes. Treat unexplained chains as a blocking risk.
5. Abuse history: screen historical/current evidence for parked, adult, gambling, pharma, malware, hacked pages, counterfeit, spam, or other abuse signals. Preserve source URLs and retrieval times, not raw archived HTML.
6. Backlinks: assess referring-domain relevance, editorial quality, anchors, language, concentration, sitewide patterns, paid/link-network signals, and toxic destinations. Authority metrics are corroboration, never approval.
7. Trademark and legacy-name risk: search the name, historical operator, logos, and marks in relevant Israeli and international sources. A legacy business name or confusing similarity blocks use until legal clearance.
8. Ownership/legal chain: identify the actual current owner, permitted acquisition path, transfer terms, and any content, brand, client, or creditor claims. No scraping of registrant PII.
9. Decision record: produce reject, hold, or manual_due_diligence_required only. Eitan manually approves naming, ownership, legal clearance, purchase, transfer, and launch.

## Hogegim commercial model

Recommend a simple 60-day pilot: fixed rental for the agreed site/category territory, with shared routing between the two clients and a written quality review. If pricing is not yet acceptable, use a capped qualified-lead fee as the fallback; do not start with uncapped per-lead billing.

Routing must be fair: alternate eligible leads by deterministic rotation, with temporary capacity pauses recorded and no manual cherry-picking. A duplicate is the same person or business, same service need, and same contact window; deduplicate within 30 days and route only the original eligible record. A qualified lead has consent to be contacted, a valid contact method, a relevant event/catering need, a service area served by the client, and a plausible date or planning window. Spam, vendors, job seekers, outside-area requests, and missing consent are excluded.

Set a response expectation, for example acknowledgement within 15 minutes during agreed hours and first human attempt within one business hour; Eitan and clients must confirm the actual SLA. Attribute form, call, and click-to-call leads with first-party event IDs, source/category/area, timestamps, and consent state. Use a 30-day initial attribution window, then report contacted, qualified, booked, won, and estimated value. Report aggregates and pseudonymous lead IDs only; clients see their own records, while portfolio reports show totals, rates, ranges, and confidence without names, phone numbers, contract terms, or confidential revenue.

## Geography and reusable architecture

Use a national information architecture, but a city-first commercial rollout. Start Hogegim with the cities/regions where the two clients actually serve and where supply can be verified; the initial shortlist is Tel Aviv metro, Jerusalem, Haifa/Krayot, and the Sharon only after provider coverage is confirmed. Do not publish thin city doorway pages. A location page needs unique, useful evidence: actual providers or venues, service boundaries, event-size fit, contact/availability data, and editorial guidance.

Reusable architecture: home/category hub, service-intent pages, evidence-backed location pages, provider/venue profiles with permission, comparison or planning guides, FAQ, lead form/call layer, consent and attribution layer, internal-link graph, XML sitemap, structured data, Search Console/analytics instrumentation, and an operator evidence ledger. Every niche remains independently useful; related links appear only where a customer task genuinely connects them, such as venue-to-catering planning or gate installation-to-maintenance. No artificial network.

## Shared Site-Bot operating architecture

Unattended, bounded work: discover and score expired candidates; collect dated evidence; research keywords/entities; produce page briefs; draft generation jobs; suggest internal links; run technical SEO checks; monitor Search Console and analytics; capture consented leads; apply deterministic routing; aggregate reports; detect indexing, response-time, duplicate, and conversion anomalies; and create recurring optimization recommendations.

Approval-gated work: publish or materially update pages, change SEO anchors, alter client-facing offers, accept providers, send client communications, spend provider credits above an approved budget, purchase/transfer domains, deploy, change provider credentials, and affect any client account or ad spend. Research outputs can only be reject, hold, or manual_due_diligence_required. Keep provider budgets, provenance, confidence, contradictions, and freshness visible.

## Proof metrics

Minimum proof is a cohort over an agreed period: indexed eligible pages; impressions and query coverage; qualified organic leads; form/call attribution completeness; response time; booked consultations/jobs; revenue or estimated pipeline range; lead-to-sale rate where available; acquisition, content, provider, and maintenance cost; operator hours; and confidence/uncertainty. Traffic is diagnostic only, never proof. A site earns expansion only when lead quality and commercial outcomes are positive enough for the client to continue, with missing-data limitations explicitly reported.
