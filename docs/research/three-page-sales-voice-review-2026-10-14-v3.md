## Material findings

1. **The copy crosses from desired outcome into a promised result.** Contract005 explicitly permits aspirational benefit language but requires removal of wording that promises guaranteed discovery or inquiry results. The homepage final headline says `הלקוח הבא שלך כבר מחפש אותך בגוגל. נוודא שהוא ימצא.` (1.10, line 134): `נוודא` asserts WAO will ensure the prospect finds the business. The Site-Bot close says `כשהעסק שלך מסודר בגוגל, הפניות מגיעות אליך` (2.12, line 282), presenting inquiries as a definite consequence. The Ads process heading promises steps “עד פניות אמיתיות בטלפון שלך” (3.5, line 332), and the Ads FAQ promises the customer will see how many inquiries arrived and their cost (3.9, line 384) without qualification. These are not just vivid aspirations; they read as guaranteed discovery/inquiry outcomes. Preserve the desired benefit, but remove the certainty that WAO can ensure it or that leads necessarily arrive. The broader H1 and benefit-led positioning can remain.

2. **Long, multi-clause sentences undermine Hebrew TTS comprehension.** The stated narration target is 12–15 words, but many spoken body/FAQ sentences are far longer and contain stacked clauses, transitions, and multiple claims. Examples include homepage body (1.3, line 42), the Site-Bot package description (2.6, line 220), its domain-choice explanation (line 229), the long price FAQ answer (2.10, line 270), and Ads hero (3.3, line 313) and Ads outcome FAQ (3.9, line 386). As written, these require listeners to retain several ideas before reaching the sentence end; punctuation alone does not create clear spoken units. Split the customer narration into short, single-idea sentences and check the spoken flow. This is a delivery/comprehension issue, not a preference for shorter visual copy.
