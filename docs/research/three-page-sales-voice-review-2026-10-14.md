## Material findings and required outcomes

1. **The Ads narrative repeatedly uses prohibited contrast and negation scaffolding.** Lines 326, 328–337, 345–350 and 365 frame the offer with constructions such as “not promises,” “not budget,” “not clicks,” “not gut feeling,” “not ownership,” and “not what we didn't check.” The repeated device makes the page sound defensive and directly violates the no not-X-but-Y / what-we-do-don't-do / reassurance-framework requirement. Replace the narrative structure with affirmative, concrete customer value and evidence-based process; do not fix this through isolated word swaps. Also reassess lines 96, 123, 125, 190, 210 and 258: stacked “without/no/never” assurances pull the copy toward caveat-led persuasion.

2. **The Ads promise implies complete per-inquiry attribution without support.** Line 69 says each inquiry is linked to the ad that brought it; lines 307 and 332–333 say every inquiry or call can be measured/identified exactly, and line 348 repeats that breadth. The source ledger establishes a target offer, not verified coverage of every call, WhatsApp message, and form or a live attribution setup. Narrow these statements to only the specific tracking capability that is actually established, or keep them out of customer copy until verified. The same issue affects Site-Bot lines 223 and 264, which promise a monthly report saying who called and where inquiries came from, while lines 83–85 separately describe call tracking and lead management as paid extensions. Reconcile the included report with those separately priced extensions; do not promise call-source reporting in the base retainer without evidence.

3. **Site-Bot describes the checklist as broader or more conclusive than its stated evidence.** Lines 119 and 266 say the report checks “the whole picture” in Google. Lines 195–202 present its six dimensions as exactly what the customer sees in Google and make claims about relevance, customer perception, and whether Google understands the business. The specified audit is an available-information profile checklist, with unknowns explicitly first-class; it is not a comprehensive view of ranking, visibility, customer choice, or Google's understanding. Describe only the six observable fields and distinguish observable data from inference/unknowns. Keep the six dimensions and useful DIY categories/hours/photos, but remove the totalizing interpretation.

4. **The “all included” and price claims conceal unresolved commercial facts.** Site-Bot line 224 says “one price, everything included,” although the ledger leaves third-party costs and the relationship to separate services unresolved; the recurring bundle itself is recorded as a target, not verified delivery. Ads line 356 says ₪249 is “all that is paid to us,” while line 357 makes a potentially broader customer-cost impression and the ledger leaves VAT and first-month terms unresolved. State only the approved management price and that media budget is paid separately to Google; do not imply total cost, all-inclusive coverage, or that no other costs apply until those terms are verified. Lines 127, 268 and 382 say terms are available/in the terms of use while cancellation and the site's fate on cancellation are explicitly unresolved. Do not direct a buyer to terms as though these material terms are settled; keep them out of asserted copy until confirmed.

5. **The free consultation is asserted as a definite 30-minute offer without adequate evidence.** Lines 308, 387–388 and 391 explicitly promise a free 30-minute consultation. The ledger's line 429 labels it current because it appears on the existing page, but inherited source copy is not operational proof, and the contract forbids inventing an SLA/terms or treating source wording as evidence. Verify that this consultation is genuinely offered on these terms before retaining it; otherwise use a CTA whose action and commitment are confirmed. Do not present the draft's own ledger as validation.

6. **Free-report CTAs route to the wrong immediate action.** Header and homepage report CTAs at lines 30, 41, 63, 133, and Site-Bot report CTAs at 189 and 278 all point to `/site-bot/start`. The packet's own ledger at 455 says this currently serves the legacy ₪9.90 trial intake, not a free report-first flow. The requested first action is the ungated report; a visitor following these buttons could instead encounter a paid commitment. The copy must not go forward with these destinations: route the report CTA to the actual free audit entry only after its availability and behavior are confirmed, or mark publication blocked until that destination exists. This finding is based on the packet's recorded implementation evidence, not a fresh runtime check.

7. **The report journey is framed as an immediate result in a few minutes despite the timeline restriction.** Line 277 says the free report “takes a few minutes,” while ledger line 451 says no supported timing claim exists. Remove the duration claim unless measured and approved. The report CTA must also not imply the report is currently obtainable at its linked destination (finding 6).

8. **Some desire-led endpoints sell a slogan rather than the customer's desired outcome.** Homepage closing line 131 (“let's see what Google says about you”) and Site-Bot closing line 276 return to the report/check as the central payoff. This undercuts the brief's explicit instruction that the free report is an acquisition experience, not the dream. Re-anchor the close in suitable inquiries, worthwhile work, or reduced marketing overhead, with the report described only as the first useful step—and only when its destination is real.

9. **One Site-Bot line is an implausible, TTS-disruptive promise.** Line 240 says the site “comes back to life” (“קם לתחייה”) as the monthly service continues. This is an unnatural, melodramatic metaphor for a business website and obscures the actual recurring outcome. Replace the concept with a plain, specific description of the verified ongoing work; do not claim the work is already operational merely to make the sentence concrete.
