# Animated-humour YouTube ads for WAO: Contabo study, original concept, production plan

**Date:** 2026-11-12 · **For:** Eitan · **Status:** strategy proposal, nothing built
**Scope:** Ads Bot and Site Bot, Hebrew, YouTube Shorts 9:16 and in-stream 16:9, 6–30s

**Verdict:** Yes, it is worth a one-week pilot, but Contabo's ads are not viral. They are a cheap, repeatable mascot format pushed with a very large media budget. What transfers to WAO is the format economics (one character, many cheap cuts) and the "product enters as the punchline" structure. Their reach does not transfer, and our trust problem is much bigger than theirs.

**Evidence labels**
- **[Verified]** = pulled today from a primary source (Google Ads Transparency Center, YouTube's own page data, vendor docs, Google policy pages).
- **[Third-party]** = reported by blogs or review sites; confirm before spending.
- **[Inference]** = my reading of the evidence.

### Format and character [Verified from stills]
- **Character:** a server unit with a face, thin robot limbs and metal boots. The ad description calls it "ContaBro: our new mascot with attitude". Contabo uses the same name for its support chatbot (Contabo blog, 2025-06-05).
- **Animation style:** flat 2D cut-out puppet with a small set of facial expressions, over painted backgrounds that look AI-generated [Inference]. It is cheap to produce and easy to re-cut.
- **Humour mechanic:** the product itself is the comedian, dropped into unrelated scenes and costumes:
  - a caped space-villain look with a red light sword in a sci-fi corridor;
  - a disco party where it becomes a mirror ball;
  - a restaurant table with one carrot on the plate and a menu full of dollar signs.
- **Captions:** one-word captions appear on screen ("PARTYING...").
- **Structure:** brand logo on screen in every frame I saw; a string of quick sight gags; a comic-book wipe; an end card with "HIGH POWER, LOW PRICES." and the URL.
- **The space-villain scene is a pop-culture pastiche.** That is their legal risk to carry, not a technique for us.

### Frequency and variations [Verified]
- One mascot campaign uploaded in April 2025 is still running 18 months later.
- It exists in landscape, vertical and square, in cuts from 15s to 30s, with at least one German-titled cut.
- Extra cuts titled "Shortened:" and "Voiceover:" sit on a Google-named "Video ad upload channel". This suggests Google Ads' own trimming and voice-over tools made them [Inference].
- In the sample, most creatives and most distinct videos were **not** the mascot. They were 10–15s price and promo cards in several currencies and languages. One price card alone had 30 separate creatives.

### Transferable principles
1. **One owned character, kept for years.** Recognition compounds and each new cut costs little.
2. **The product is the punchline, not an interruption.** There is no "and now a word from our sponsor" gear change.
3. **Brand visible throughout.** Skippers still see who it was.
4. **Jokes that work on mute.** Sight gags and one-word captions, not dialogue.
5. **One master, many cuts.** Three aspect ratios and several lengths from the same scenes.
6. **Humour builds the memory; plain offer cards close.** They run both side by side.
7. **A cheap animation style is a feature.** It makes volume and consistency affordable.

**We do not copy:** the server-with-a-face design, the limbs, the palette, the genre parodies, the slogan, the transitions or any audio.

## 2. Fit for WAO

### Why it could work
- **The audience is there.** The Israel Internet Association's 2026 survey puts YouTube use among Israeli adults at 98% [Third-party report of the survey; confirm in the ISOC-IL release].
- **The enemy lends itself to a sight gag.** Clicks that cost money and never ring is an easy thing to make visible and funny. We would be dramatising the tradesman's own problem, not inventing one.
- **A character solves a real production problem.** We may not show real clients or testimonials. A fictional cartoon world shows the pain vividly with no real person in it.
- **Recurring characters have evidence behind them.** System1's analysis of UK advertising-effectiveness data reports that campaigns with a recurring character are more likely to gain market share. Summaries give figures between 23% and 38%, so quote none of them publicly [Third-party].
- **It fits how WAO already works.** Scripted, repeatable, pipeline-built video is what the course engine does.

### Why it could fail
- **We cannot buy Contabo's frequency.** Their recognition comes from tens of millions of paid views. At WAO's budget the character must earn its keep inside a single viewing.
- **It is a different purchase.** Contabo sells a $5 server to developers who can verify specs. We ask a plumber to hand over his ad budget and his lead flow. A cartoon alone reads as "app for kids" or as one more faceless online operator.
- **The joke can land on the wrong target.** If it makes the tradesman look foolish for wasting money, we lose him. The joke must be on the clicks, the reports and the invisibility.
- **Hebrew comedy timing is fragile.** Translated or AI-flavoured Hebrew kills a joke. This is Tamar's work with Noa's gate, and voiced Hebrew needs your ear.
- **A low-effort AI look costs trust.** Tradesmen judge workmanship for a living, and visible AI wobble signals a sloppy supplier.

### Trust risks and the fix
| Risk | Fix |
|---|---|
| "Who are these people?" | Every ad ends in the real world: your real recorded voice (not a clone), name and role on screen. |
| "Is this a real product?" | The last seconds show a real product screen, captured from the live product. |
| "Sounds too good" | The character states the pain; the product line states the mechanism, never an outcome. |
| "Cartoon = not serious" | Fixed rule: the animated world shows only the problem. The solution is always real screens or real footage. |

Recommended split for a 15s ad: about 10s cartoon problem, about 5s real screen plus founder voice. For 30s: about 20s and 10s, with you on camera for 2–3 seconds as an option to test.

## 3. Our own concept: "The phone that doesn't ring"

Names are English placeholders. The Hebrew name, every joke and every spoken line are Tamar's (via waocopy), then Noa, then your spot-check.

### The character
- **Who:** the tradesman's own work phone. Rugged, scuffed, in a thick orange rubber case with plaster dust in the corners and a hairline crack across one corner. Working name: **Dash**, because it lives on a magnet mount on the van's dashboard.
- **Face:** two eyes and a mouth drawn on its screen, like a simple display animation.
- **Personality:** deadpan, loyal, slightly tired. It sees every click, every search and every missed customer. It never mocks its owner; it mocks what is being done to him.
- **Signature device:** its jokes arrive as cartoon notification banners on its own screen, so every joke also reads on mute.

### The world
- **Place:** the dashboard of a work van, parked between jobs. Coffee cup, receipts, a spirit level, a jar of coins.
- **The owner:** never fully seen. Hands, forearms, a tool belt, a voice from off-screen. Any plumber, electrician or AC technician can see himself. Singular male address.
- **The enemies, made visible:**
  - Ads Bot: **click-flies**, small cursor-shaped insects that each eat a coin and leave without a sound.
  - Site Bot: **the ghost van**. The van and its owner are see-through to everyone searching on the pavement.

### Why this design
- **The phone is the scoreboard.** Both bots are judged by whether the phone rings.
- **Real product screens appear naturally** on a phone, so the trust element is built into the character.
- **It is cheap and consistent to animate.** A rigid object with a screen-face has no body to drift between episodes. Its mouth can be driven directly by the audio track, which removes the hardest technical risk (Hebrew lip-sync).
- **It looks nothing like Contabo's mascot** and borrows from no film or game.

### Episode format
- **Hook (0–3s):** one impossible image plus one notification. Brand mark in the corner from the first frame.
- **Escalation:** the same gag, twice, bigger.
- **Turn:** Dash says the true thing plainly.
- **Product entry:** Dash's screen switches from cartoon face to the real product screen. Your real voice delivers one line. End card.
- **Never:** the phone ringing off the hook at the end, a counter of leads, or a "before and after". Those imply a result.

### Ads Bot episodes (enemy: paying for clicks that never ring)

**A1. "The meter"**
- **Hook (0–3s):** Dash's screen is a taxi meter. Each click sound adds shekels. Dash stares at the camera, silent. Notification: another click, no call.
- **15s beats:**
  - 0–3: hook.
  - 3–7: the meter speeds up; coins leave the jar on their own.
  - 7–10: the owner's hand picks Dash up, checks for missed calls, finds none. Dash delivers the turn (intent: "they all clicked, nobody rang").
  - 10–15: product entry and end card.
- **30s beats:**
  - 0–3: hook.
  - 3–10: three quick clicks, each shown as a cursor that looks around and leaves.
  - 10–16: the owner asks who these people were; Dash shrugs.
  - 16–20: the turn.
  - 20–27: real product screen, a lead being marked as real by the owner's thumb.
  - 27–30: end card with price.
- **Product enters on:** intent "Ads Bot learns from the leads you mark as real, not from clicks."

**A2. "Click-flies"**
- **Hook (0–3s):** a cloud of cursor-shaped flies circles the coin jar. Dash swats one with its own charging cable.
- **15s beats:**
  - 0–3: hook.
  - 3–8: every fly takes a coin and leaves; Dash counts them out loud, bored.
  - 8–10: the turn (intent: "lots of visitors, zero customers").
  - 10–15: product entry.
- **30s beats:**
  - 0–3: hook.
  - 3–9: the swarm grows; the owner sprays, they come back.
  - 9–15: one fly stops, looks like it might call, then leaves.
  - 15–20: the turn.
  - 20–27: real screen, the owner marks which enquiries were real jobs.
  - 27–30: end card.
- **Product enters on:** intent "tell it which calls were real work, and it steers the budget toward more like those."

**A3. "The report"**
- **Hook (0–3s):** a glossy report slaps onto the windscreen from outside, full of big green arrows. Dash reads it upside down.
- **15s beats:**
  - 0–3: hook.
  - 3–8: confetti for "clicks" and "impressions"; Dash asks which line is a customer; the confetti stops in mid-air.
  - 8–10: the turn.
  - 10–15: product entry with price.
- **30s beats:**
  - 0–3: hook.
  - 3–10: three reports pile up, each shinier.
  - 10–16: the owner asks how many called; silence, one cricket.
  - 16–20: the turn.
  - 20–27: real screen plus the price line.
  - 27–30: end card.
- **Product enters on:** intent "one number that matters: leads you confirmed. ₪249 + VAT a month."
- **Check before use:** any comparison to agency prices ("typically from about ₪1,500") must be worded as typical, be supportable with evidence, and get Dror's review. Put it on screen as text, not in the joke.

### Site Bot episodes (enemy: invisible on Google despite great work and reviews)

**S1. "Ghost van"**
- **Hook (0–3s):** a woman on the pavement searches on her phone and walks straight through the see-through van. Dash watches her pass.
- **15s beats:**
  - 0–3: hook.
  - 3–8: two more people walk through; one puts a coffee down "on" the bonnet and it falls.
  - 8–10: the turn (intent: "years of good work, and they cannot see us").
  - 10–15: product entry.
- **30s beats:**
  - 0–3: hook.
  - 3–9: the walk-throughs.
  - 9–15: the owner waves; nobody reacts; Dash notes that someone two streets away got the job.
  - 15–20: the turn.
  - 20–27: real screens, the service-tree site next to the business profile, saying the same thing.
  - 27–30: end card.
- **Product enters on:** intent "Site Bot builds your full presence on Google: a site that mirrors every service you do, in step with your business profile."

**S2. "The jar of stars"**
- **Hook (0–3s):** the glovebox opens and a jar of glowing stars rolls out. Notification: compliments received in person, none written anywhere.
- **15s beats:**
  - 0–3: hook.
  - 3–8: each star replays a customer's thank-you at the door as a speech bubble with no sound.
  - 8–10: the turn (intent: "Google has no ears").
  - 10–15: product entry.
- **30s beats:**
  - 0–3: hook.
  - 3–10: the owner adds another star after a job.
  - 10–16: Dash holds the jar up to the windscreen; passers-by cannot see it.
  - 16–20: the turn.
  - 20–27: real screen, the flow that helps a customer write a review.
  - 27–30: end card.
- **Product enters on:** intent "we help your customers put it in writing, where the next customer looks."
- **Check before use:** the customers and their words are generic and fictional, and must never look like quoted testimonials. Yonatan confirms the wording stays inside Google's review rules.

**S3. "The level"**
- **Hook (0–3s):** a spirit level on the dashboard, bubble jammed to one side. Dash shows three labels for the same business, each different.
- **15s beats:**
  - 0–3: hook.
  - 3–8: the site says one trade, the profile another, a customer describes a third; the bubble slides further each time.
  - 8–10: the turn (intent: "three stories, so Google trusts none").
  - 10–15: product entry as the bubble centres.
- **30s beats:**
  - 0–3: hook.
  - 3–10: the three mismatches, one by one.
  - 10–16: the owner straightens a shelf by instinct but cannot straighten this.
  - 16–20: the turn.
  - 20–27: real screens, one service tree with site and profile aligned; the bubble centres.
  - 27–30: end card.
- **Product enters on:** intent "one tree of services, the same story on your site and your profile."

**Positioning guard for all three:** Site Bot is the complete organic Google exposure system for a one-man business. Never "a small website", never a page count, never a ranking promise.

## 4. Production: tools, costs, pilot

### Voice (Hebrew)
- **ElevenLabs' current model is Eleven v4** (`eleven_v4`), launched 2026-09-28 with 90+ languages. Hebrew is listed in the official docs. Eleven v3 is now labelled previous generation and also lists Hebrew. [Verified: ElevenLabs docs; launch date from TechCrunch]
- **Hebrew quality is unproven.** I found no independent Hebrew evaluation of v3 or v4. ElevenLabs' own v4 page says to test your specific voice and language, that audio tags are "not perfect yet", and that v4 has no speed control. [Verified]
- **Our own script is a version behind.** `scripts/generate-preview-audio.js` calls `eleven_v3`. The pilot should compare v4 against v3 on the same lines.
- **Known Hebrew trap:** unpointed Hebrew has words that can be read two ways. Plan for retakes and your ear on every final line.
- **Plans:** Starter $6/month (30,000 credits) and Creator $22/month (121,000 credits) both include a commercial licence. [Verified: pricing page]
- **Founder tag:** your real recorded voice, not a synthetic one. That is the trust element.

### Why the phone character changes the tool choice
- **Lip-sync:** Dash's mouth is a shape on a screen. Our own video assembly script can open and close it from the loudness of the audio. No AI lip-sync model is needed, and it is identical in every episode.
- **Character consistency:** Dash is a fixed set of layered drawings, not something an AI redraws each time. AI video is used only for background gag shots, where small drift does not matter.
- **Hebrew captions:** we already have the exact script, so our assembly script places the captions, not speech recognition. I could not confirm native Hebrew caption support in CapCut, and Descript has a feature request for Hebrew and right-to-left support (status not checked). [Third-party]

### Recommended stack
1. **Script:** waocopy (Tamar) → Noa → your spot-check of voiced lines.
2. **Character art:** generate options with the Gemini image model, then pay a human illustrator once to redraw the chosen design as clean layers. A brand character should have a human author and clean ownership; confirm the copyright point with a lawyer.
3. **Voice:** ElevenLabs v4 for Dash (compared with v3); your real voice for the closing line.
4. **Gag shots:** Gemini Omni Flash through the existing `GEMINI_API_KEY`, using the character sheet as reference.
5. **Assembly, captions, end cards:** a scripted step in the repo using ffmpeg, as the course pipeline already does. Maya checks right-to-left rendering, including mixed Hebrew and Latin.
6. **Real product screens:** captured from the live product on a test account. No client data.
7. **Fallback if the rig feels stiff:** Hedra Character-3 for talking shots, after reading its terms on advertising.

Steps 3–5 can be scripted, so an episode becomes a script file plus a render command rather than a manual edit.

### Versus an Israeli animation freelancer
- **Published prices** [Third-party; vendor sales pages, some dated, none freelancer-specific]: simple 2D 30s from about ₪900–1,800; "AI animation" from about ₪1,100 per 30s; 3D from about ₪3,800 per 30s. A custom recurring character with a rig will cost more; get quotes.
- **Human strengths:** comic timing, a clean look, clear ownership.
- **Human weaknesses for us:** every new cut, length and aspect ratio is a new invoice and a new wait. The Contabo lesson is volume of cuts.
- **Recommendation:** hybrid. Pay a human once for character design and layered artwork, and produce episodes in-house.

**Pass / fail (all must pass):**
1. **Hebrew voice:** you and Noa find zero mispronunciations in the final cut, and no line needed more than five retakes.
2. **Mute test:** five viewers watch without sound; at least four can say what the problem is.
3. **Product test:** with sound, at least four of five can say what the bot does, and none describes it as a promise of results.
4. **Trust test:** none of the five says it looks like a scam, a game or a children's app; at least three say they would watch another.
5. **Consistency:** Dash is visibly the same character in both films, side by side.
6. **Right-to-left:** zero caption or mixed-direction text defects in Maya's check on a phone.
7. **Repeatability:** a third episode can be produced from a finished script in one working day for under $30 in tools.
8. **Policy:** every item in section 5 is ticked.

**If it fails:** keep the scripts and the character, and either hand animation to a freelancer or shoot the same jokes with you and a real phone.

## 5. Policies that touch humorous and animated ads

All Google pages were fetched 2026-11-12. The two policy pages (Misrepresentation, Inappropriate content) showed no dated change notice.

**Google Ads: Misrepresentation** [Verified]
- **Unreliable claims:** no "inaccurate claims or claims that entice the user with an improbable result", even if that result is possible. For us: no implied flood of calls, no ringing-phone finale.
- **Clickbait:** no "clickbait tactics or sensationalist text or imagery", and no use of negative life events such as bankruptcy to pressure the viewer. For us: the joke is wasted clicks, not a business going under.
- **Misleading ad design:** no designs that mimic system notifications or fake buttons. This matters most for Dash. Its banners must be obviously cartoon, inside the cartoon phone, and never resemble the viewer's own phone alerts. No realistic ringtone or vibration sound in the first seconds.
- **Unavailable offers:** whatever the ad promises must be easy to find on the landing page. For us: the price on screen must match the page, and no Google ad-credit amount is mentioned.

**Google Ads: Inappropriate content** [Verified]
- No obscene or profane language, nothing designed to shock or scare, no bullying or disparagement.
- Content that looks family-friendly but carries adult themes is prohibited. For us: clean language in the jokes, and no mocking of a recognisable competitor or type of person.

**AI labelling** [Verified: Google policy update posted 2026-07-09]
- Since July 2026 Google allows a text or visual AI label on image and video ads. It is optional under Google's own policy.
- Google names the EU, India and New York as places whose laws require such disclosure in some cases. Israel is not named. The mandatory "altered or synthetic content" checkbox applies to election ads only.

**YouTube Shorts ad format** [Verified: Google's Shorts ads guide]
- Vertical 9:16 is prioritised. For action-oriented campaigns Google recommends 10–30s; reach campaigns accept 6–60s.
- Only the first 60 seconds play in the Shorts feed.
- The call-to-action button appears at 3 seconds in Demand Gen and Performance Max, and at 10 seconds in video view and reach campaigns.
- Google says adding sound (music, voice or both) raises conversions by over 20%. Headline 40 characters, description 90.

**Not verified today; check before launch**
- **Google's brand and review rules:** keep maps, search pages and star ratings generic and hand-drawn, with no Google logos or interface. Yonatan confirms the review-help wording against Google's review policy.
- **Israeli Consumer Protection Law, section 7 (misleading advertising):** an Attorney General's opinion applies it to ads in any medium [Third-party summary]. The agency-price comparison needs evidence on file. One check with a lawyer covers this and the character-ownership question.

**WAO's own hard lines, mapped**
| Hard line | How the concept keeps it |
|---|---|
| No real client examples | Fictional cartoon world; product screens from a test account |
| No results guarantee | Product lines describe the mechanism; no ringing finale, no counters |
| No Google credit amount | Not mentioned anywhere |
| Nothing presented as a testimonial | The owner is faceless and fictional; customer "stars" are generic, never quotes |
